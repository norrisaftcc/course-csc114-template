04 / The framework

Before you write a word, answer three questions.

QUESTION 1Who are you?
What role does Claude play?
QUESTION 2Who am I?
Who is being helped — and what do they already know?
QUESTION 3What will we do together?
What's the goal — and what should Claude not do?
05 / Worked example

Three short paragraphs. That's the whole prompt.

Who are you?
You are a study assistant for CSC-113. You explain concepts clearly and patiently using the material I give you.
Who am I?
I'm a student who attended the lectures. I know the basics but get stuck on details. Use plain language. Don't assume I'm an expert.
What together?
We clarify confusing topics by Q&A. Use only my uploaded notes. If something isn't covered, say so. Never make up an answer.
06 / The rule that matters

One rule does the heavy lifting: "use only my notes."

✗ Without the rule
You ask about something your notes never mention…
"The exam is worth 30% of your grade."
(It made that up. It sounds right. It's wrong.)
✓ With the rule
Same question, same missing info…
"That isn't in the notes you gave me."

That rule gives the AI permission to say "I don't know." It's the line between a tool you can trust and one that confidently misleads you.

07 / Prove it works

Test it three ways.

Known good
Ask something your notes clearly answer.
Does it give the right answer, from your material?
should SUCCEED
Known bad
Ask something your notes don't cover at all.
Does it admit it doesn't know — or bluff?
should be REFUSED
Edge case
Ask something in the gray area — partly covered.
What does it do at the boundary of what it knows?
for DISCOVERY
08 / How to fix it

When something's off, change one thing.

Change five things at once and you won't know which one fixed it. One change, one re-test, one observation. (Yes — the same rule from CSC-113.)
